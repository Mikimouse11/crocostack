# Categories

- Every category is an entry point for customers. Therefore, more categories is better than too few.
- Organize the tree around how shoppers browse, not how the warehouse or supplier groups stock.
- Name categories with the words shoppers search. Be clear and specific. "Men's Running shoes", not "Footwear - Performance".
- Keep the tree at three levels or fewer. Deeper trees hide products.
- Siblings must not overlap. If a product fits two siblings equally well, the split is wrong.
- Use multiple category systems. Example: furniture stores can have systems based on product type ("Chairs", "Beds"), room ("Living Room", "Bed Room") and brand. 
- Split by product type with subcategories. Split by attribute (color, size, price) with filters.
- Merge a category with fewer than about 5 products into its parent. Delete or hide empty categories.
- Keep marketing collections such as "Sale", "New", and "Gifts" outside the main tree.
- Assign products to one most specific category per system.
- Don't add a product to a subcategory and its parent categories.
